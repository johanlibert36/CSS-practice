Article 1

In electrical engineering, physical copper traces isolate signals so that voltage on one wire does not accidentally trigger an adjacent logic gate. Inside a living bacterium, however, every genetic gate floats in the same shared cytoplasmic soup—making orthogonality, cellular load, and transcriptional read-through the central physical constraints that determine whether a synthetic genetic circuit computes reliably or collapses into biochemical noise.

Article 2

In synthetic biology, orthogonality means that discrete biological components operate independently inside the same cell without unintended cross-reactions (cross-talk). Because all transcription factors (TFs) diffuse freely through the cytoplasm, you cannot reuse the exact same transcription factor-promoter pair for two separate logic gates without their signals merging together.

Article 3

Even when every transcription factor is strictly orthogonal, scaling up a genetic circuit inside a single bacterium hits two major biophysical walls: host resource exhaustion and DNA layout interference.
